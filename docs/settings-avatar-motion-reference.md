## Mask reconstruction and evidence limits

Telegram's `UserAvatarMask.tgs` was inspected as reference. It has 540 frames at 60 fps, so a scrolling offset maps to frame `4.5 × offset`. Its initial circular layer joins a top connection shape around frame 153 (offset 34), and by frame 498 (offset 110.7) only the thin connection strip remains. The connection is not the hardware island's capsule outline.

The round photo keeps its own circular clip and scale; a separate 171-point effect canvas fills the connection outside the photo. Black backing, photo, blur, radial edge and fade are jointly masked, with an outer top clip at 47 points. The radial artwork remains 100×100, horizontally centered at the effect canvas top; enlarging it to 171×171 displaces the clear center from y=88 to y=150.48 and darkens the wrong area. The title/photo collapse fraction is separately derived from font metrics: clamp(offset / (107 + floor(ascender − descender)/2)) for this header's nonempty subtitle. It must not be replaced by the mask's offset/120 fraction.

Settings disables SwiftUI's automatic top scroll-edge effect with `scrollEdgeEffectHidden(true, for: .top)` (iOS 26+). Otherwise a second full-width surface can cover the title and avatar even with the navigation-bar background hidden. The navigation toolbar retains an accessible name without rendering a duplicate title.

The app uses independently constructed Bézier geometry for those stages. A separate hanging connection grows into the photo before the joined contour takes over; shoulder width and height are independent of the lower body's radius, allowing the final shallow bowl to retract into the top strip. The reference's topology, placement, progress and effects guide it, but it is not the original TGS vector and must not be described as a pixel-identical copy.

## Compact-island contact

The original 96.8-point strip sits 0.5 points below the broad rounded clipping container's top. That container widens rapidly just below its top edge, exposing thin fins when the hardware no longer covers the strip ends. iPhone 18 Pro / Pro Max retain the original flared, concave shoulder, with a horizontal inward first tangent and shared neck/body tangents. Their connection is closed directly across its root; it has no separate strip, shelf or vertical end caps. Root half-width is the smaller of the original 45.7-point lip and the software clip's W/10 flat half-width, inset by one display pixel. A fixed 27-point half-width and vertical tangent are inappropriate: the authored late neck reaches 30.6 points, so that shape bulges outward from the root and forms a bucket-like shoulder.

The root begins one display pixel above the outer clip. During the final shallow-bowl retreat, it contracts toward the existing neck as the lower body falls below 20 points high. Root overscan also fades as the remaining connection drops below two display pixels, so the empty final path has zero area instead of a separate visible cap. Fully expanded photos use zero morph strength, leaving the regular photo and name extension available. The original body phases, motion, text and older-model clipping remain in place.

This width follows the existing software clip rather than a guessed hardware rectangle. UIKit's public safe-area and status-bar APIs do not report cutout width. The compact profile is limited to verified identifiers `iPhone19,2`, `iPhone19,3`, and `iPhone19,7`, from [DeviceKit's device mapping](https://github.com/devicekit/DeviceKit/blob/master/Source/Device.generated.swift); the Apple references are [18 Pro](https://support.apple.com/en-us/148590) and [18 Pro Max](https://support.apple.com/en-us/148591). Simulators use `SIMULATOR_MODEL_IDENTIFIER`. Other identifiers retain the original profile. The composite shape mask uses the current renderer's display scale for its Retina edge.

A smooth mask alone does not produce a smooth painted edge. The backing, blur, black cover and radial view originally all began at canvas y=47.5, cutting off the mask's overscanned pixels. Around offset 42, the photo covers only about 9 points on either side of the center above this line while the shoulder spreads farther outward; starting the effect paint there creates a horizontal ledge. Compact profiles extend all four paint layers upward by 2 points plus one display pixel under the same composite mask. The canvas remains 171×171 at y=47.5. The radial view's extended frame and drawing translation cancel, preserving its 100-point artwork and center at canvas y=88. Legacy profiles have zero paint overscan.

On a short landscape viewport, the independently adapted expanded square is capped at viewportHeight−60 and centered horizontally so the name and subtitle remain visible. Portrait phones with sufficient height retain the screen-width square. The same side drives the photo, text and scroll placeholder; this is a responsive adaptation, not a claim about Telegram's landscape layout.

The header placeholder and visible renderer share the explicit expansion value. Geometry measurements establish the stable content origin and window dimensions; they do not infer expansion from an animated image height. The gallery asks the actual image renderer for its current window-space frame, and asks again on return after the covered header restores the circle.

## Acceptance

Build success, reducer tests, and visual/device acceptance are distinct evidence. Preserve the existing gesture reducer tests. On a Dynamic Island iPhone compare recordings for slow/reversed upward scrolling, the neck-to-bulb join near offset 34, expansion while the finger remains down, release bounce, the second pull, cancelled viewer dismissal, and return after rotation. Verify Reduced Motion, an avatar without a photo, and dragging from the photo itself. These visual checks have not been established by source review.

Also compare the name throughout the scroll: it must remain a single opaque label, shrink continuously, and stop moving at its lock point while the subtitle fades. Check that no full-width top scroll-edge panel appears. The supplied Telegram and eSheep+ screenshots establish the previous duplicate-name and top-surface defects; they do not establish acceptance of the updated renderer.

For the compact profile, inspect both sides of the island's lower edge on 18 Pro and Pro Max during slow scrolling and reverse scrolling, including the final retreat. Compare an older island model, Reduced Motion, rotation and full-photo expansion. The unit tests cover flared shoulder width within the software clip, horizontal inward root tangents, shared neck/body tangents, zero-area final retreat, legacy control points, model selection, full-photo expansion, paint coverage at the offset-42 regression, all three effect-layer frames and radial drawing above the old canvas edge. Hardware alignment and Core Animation edge quality still require device acceptance.
